Abstract

We document the one positive, reproducible result from 19V Capital’s Bitcoin / Deribit-options research program: the month-start 30-day at-the-money implied volatility (ATM IV) of Deribit Bitcoin options is a strong, era-stable forecast of Bitcoin’s realized volatility over the subsequent 21 trading days. In a pre-registered, seed-reproducible study, ATM IV attains a Spearman rank information coefficient of +0.66 (Newey-West t = +6.69, n = 60), clears the strictest multiple-testing bar across a 60-signal family, holds its sign across every observable market era, and statistically subsumes trailing realized volatility — the obvious competing predictor adds essentially nothing once implied vol is included.

A directional hypothesis — that the surface predicts the sign of forward returns — was pre-registered and rejected. A companion application backtest shows that sizing a monthly volatility-targeted Bitcoin exposure on ATM IV tracks the volatility target materially tighter, trades cheaper, and roughly halves drawdown relative to an unsized position. The signal’s value is therefore as a volatility / risk conditioner — position sizing, regime-gating, vol-targeting — rather than as a directional alpha source. All results are research-grade; the binding out-of-sample validation pass remains outstanding.

1. Executive summary

This dossier documents the one genuinely positive, reproducible result from 19V Capital’s Bitcoin / Deribit-options research effort. Two studies were run, both at research fidelity, both pre-registered (hypotheses and decision rules fixed before results were seen), both seeded for exact reproduction.

  • Study 1 (signal study). Establishes the core fact: Deribit month-start 30-day ATM implied volatility (IV) is a strong, era-stable forecast of forward realized volatility (RV), and it subsumes trailing RV. The directional hypothesis (does the surface predict return direction?) was tested and killed, as expected. Verdict: ITERATE — advance the IV→RV finding as an input; do not treat it as a standalone strategy.
  • Study 2 (application backtest). Takes the forecast and tests whether it sizes a position better than the conventional trailing-RV estimate, by running a monthly volatility-targeted long-Bitcoin exposure. IV-based sizing tracks the volatility target materially tighter, runs cheaper, and roughly halves drawdown versus an unsized position. Verdict: HANDOFF — ready for the full binding validation pass.

What it is, in one line: a high-quality, literature-consistent volatility forecaster for Bitcoin — useful as a risk/sizing conditioner for executable strategies, not as a buy/sell signal. What remains: the binding out-of-sample validation pass (Section 14) has not yet been run; all numbers here are research-grade.

2. Context & the question

Bitcoin is traded by 19V on listed CME futures; an associated options market (Deribit) prices implied volatility across strikes and maturities. A standing question in the research program was whether information in the Deribit options surface — implied vol level, term-structure slope, skew, put/call open interest — carries forward-looking signal about Bitcoin that could improve an executable futures strategy.

Two distinct claims were separated up front, because they have very different prior probabilities and very different value:

  • H1 — volatility forecasting (primary). Does month-start surface information predict how much Bitcoin will move (realized volatility) over the following weeks? Prior: plausible — implied vol is, by construction, the market’s forward vol expectation, and this relationship is one of the most replicated facts in empirical finance.
  • H2 — directional forecasting (secondary). Does it predict which way Bitcoin will move (signed returns)? Prior: low — options flow is largely direction-agnostic, and any implied-minus-realized vol gap is known to be a risk premium, not a directional forecast.

The execution context matters and shapes how the result can be used. 19V’s executable account enforces intraday-flat discipline (no overnight or multi-day holds) and tight real-time loss limits. A monthly-held position is therefore not directly executable on that account; a volatility/sizing conditioner that improves an intraday strategy is. This is why the deliverable is framed as a conditioner, and why a venue-agnostic, reusable forecast is strategically valuable: it survives a change of execution venue.

3. Definitions & glossary

Every term and statistic used in this document, defined so it stands alone.

TermDefinition
Implied volatility (IV)The volatility figure implied by an option’s market price — the market’s forward-looking estimate of how much the underlying will move, annualized. “ATM” = at-the-money (strike near spot). “30-day” = constant-maturity 30 calendar days.
Realized volatility (RV)The volatility actually observed after the fact, computed from price movements. “Forward 21d RV” = the RV realized over the 21 trading days following the observation.
Trailing RVRV measured over a window ending before the observation (here, the prior 21 trading days). The standard backward-looking benchmark forecast.
Spearman ICInformation Coefficient = the rank correlation between the signal and the outcome. Ranges −1 to +1; +0.66 means a strong, monotone positive relationship robust to outliers (ranks, not raw values).
Newey-West (NW) t-statA t-statistic computed with a HAC (heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent) variance estimator. It corrects standard errors for overlapping/serially-correlated observations, which would otherwise overstate significance. “NW(2)” = 2 lags. |t| ≥ ~2 is conventionally “significant.”
Bonferroni correctionA conservative multiple-testing adjustment: if you test m signals, raise the significance bar so the chance of any false positive stays controlled. Here m=60 raises the bar to |t| ≥ 3.2.
Block bootstrapA resampling method that draws contiguous blocks of data (preserving autocorrelation) to build a confidence interval. “CI excludes zero” = the effect is unlikely to be noise. Here: 6-month blocks, 2000 resamples.
Era stabilityWhether the effect holds across distinct market regimes rather than concentrating in one period. Tested by splitting the sample (here at the Jan-2024 spot-ETF launch) and requiring same sign and independent significance in each part.
Variance risk premium (VRP)The systematic gap between implied and subsequently realized variance — compensation paid for bearing volatility risk. It explains why IV forecasts vol but not direction.
QLIKEA volatility-forecast loss function, r/f − ln(r/f) − 1 (r = realized variance, f = forecast). It penalizes under-forecasting volatility harder than over-forecasting — the appropriate loss for risk sizing. Lower is better.
Diebold-Mariano (DM) testA formal test of whether one forecast’s loss is significantly lower than another’s. A negative DM statistic vs a benchmark means the candidate forecast wins.
Pre-registrationFixing the hypotheses, signal list, sample, and decision rules in writing before computing results — the discipline that prevents fitting a story to noise.
Research fidelity vs binding“Research fidelity” = a first-pass study sufficient to decide whether to invest more. “Binding” = the full out-of-sample / walk-forward / selection-bias validation required before any capital decision.

4. Signal specification (exact formulas)

All features are computed from a single monthly snapshot — the earliest complete hourly Deribit options snapshot on the 1st of each month (typically 01:00 UTC, chosen as the first snapshot with quote count ≥ 50% of that day’s maximum). Every input is lookahead-safe (see Section 5).

InputExact construction
atm_iv_30 (headline feature)Per expiry: ATM mark-IV by linear interpolation of mark-IV at delta = +0.50 (call) and −0.50 (put), averaged (filters: ≥3 quotes/side, delta in [0.03, 0.97], time-to-expiry 2–200 days, mark-IV in (1, 500)). Constant-maturity 30-day value by linear interpolation in total variance (IV²·T) across the two bracketing expiries; flat extrapolation outside the listed range (flagged). Also computed at 7- and 90-day maturities.
Target: fwd_rv_hfwd_rv_h = sqrt( 252 · mean[ daily 5-minute realized variance ] over trading days anchor+1 … anchor+h ) where the anchor = the first Bitcoin futures trading day on/after the 1st. Horizons h ∈ {5, 10, 21} trading days. Daily realized variance = sum of squared 5-minute log returns.
Target: fwd_ret_hLog close-to-close return over h trading days from the anchor (the directional target for H2).
rv_trail_30 (benchmark)sqrt( 252 · mean[ daily 5-min realized variance ] over the last 21 trading days ending strictly BEFORE the 1st ) (≥15 days required). The backward-looking competitor to atm_iv_30.
vrp_30atm_iv_30/100 − rv_trail_30 — the (trailing) variance-risk-premium proxy.
Term-structure / skewts_slope_7_90 = atm_iv_90 − atm_iv_7; ts_slope_30_90 = atm_iv_90 − atm_iv_30; rr25_30 = 25-delta risk reversal; bf25_30 = 25-delta butterfly (all interpolated to 30 days).
Positioningpc_oi_ratio = put open-interest / call open-interest; total_oi_btc/usd, log_total_oi_usd = aggregate open interest.

5. Data & environment

  • Options data. Deribit Bitcoin options chain, hourly surface snapshots, month-start days only, 2019-10 → 2026-06 (81 monthly files), drawn from 19V’s market-data store. Structural limitation: the historical archive holds only ~one snapshot day per month, which is why the study is monthly and low-powered; continuously-running collectors now accrue daily snapshots going forward.
  • Underlying / returns & RV. CME Bitcoin futures, continuous 1-minute bars, 2021-04-09 → 2026-05-19. Daily realized variance from 5-minute log returns.
  • Usable sample. The overlap of the two datasets gives 61 monthly observations, 2021-05-01 → 2026-05-01 (60 have a complete 21-day forward window). This is the binding constraint on power: any effect with a true |IC| below ~0.25 is undetectable at this n.
  • Environment / reproducibility. macOS, Python 3.13 (pandas, numpy, matplotlib, pyarrow; HAC/bootstrap implemented directly in numpy). Single random seed 20260611 throughout; pipeline is deterministic and rebuildable end-to-end from the stored snapshots.

6. Methodology & statistical tests

Each test below is defined so the results in Sections 8–9 are interpretable without outside reference.

  • Rank correlation (IC). Spearman correlation between each feature and each target, with t ≈ IC·√((n−2)/(1−IC²)). Rank-based to neutralize Bitcoin’s fat tails.
  • Regression significance. Per-feature OLS (standardized) with Newey-West(2) t-statistics, correcting for the mild overlap of monthly observations at the 21-day horizon.
  • Multiplicity control. A family of m = 60 tests was declared in advance (10 features × 6 targets). The Bonferroni significance bar is therefore |t| ≥ 3.2; |t| in [2, 3.2] is treated as “interesting, unconfirmed.”
  • Confidence intervals. Circular block bootstrap, 6-month blocks, 2000 resamples, on the headline ICs — a CI that excludes zero is the evidence the effect is not noise.
  • Incremental / encompassing test. Bivariate regression fwd_rv ~ z(atm_iv_30) + z(rv_trail_30): if IV retains its t-statistic while trailing RV collapses, IV carries information beyond mere volatility persistence.
  • Era split. Sample divided at 2024-01-01 (Bitcoin spot-ETF launch). Each half must show the same sign and independent significance.
  • Forecast-loss tests (Study 2). QLIKE and RMSE on volatility points; Diebold-Mariano loss differentials versus the trailing-RV benchmark with Newey-West(2) t-statistics; block-bootstrap CI on the mean loss differential.

9. Study 2 — volatility-conditioner backtest

Application backtest  Verdict HANDOFF (ready for binding validation)

Because the directional edge is dead, the honest way to “backtest” the forecast is as a risk conditioner: does sizing a position with the IV forecast beat sizing it with the conventional trailing-RV estimate? The test runs a passive long-Bitcoin exposure and rescales it monthly to a fixed volatility target:

weight_t = min( 0.30 / forecast_vol_t , 1.5 ) — rebalanced each month-start; target = 30% annualized vol (20% tested as a sensitivity); cap at 1.5×.

The position is passive long Bitcoin beta — no directional claim is made. The deliverable is purely: forecast accuracy, volatility-targeting fidelity, and the risk reduction attributable to better sizing. The test compares two ways of estimating the volatility used to size the position: the implied-vol forecast (this dossier’s signal) against the conventional trailing-volatility estimate (the standard backward-looking benchmark), plus an unsized constant position as a floor.

9.3 An informative negative — bias-correcting the forecast makes it worse

A bias-corrected variant attempted to remove implied vol’s known upward level bias with a rolling regression calibration. It backfired: bias increased (to +6.5 from +3.9 vol points) and rank quality collapsed (rank correlation 0.40 versus 0.66 for the raw forecast). At this sample size the calibration is itself an overfit — it is trained on the high-volatility 2021–22 period and goes stale through the post-ETF regime shift. Conclusion: use the raw implied-vol forecast; do not bias-correct at monthly cadence.

9.4 Verdict and what remains

The pre-registered decision rule is met on all three required clauses — the primary forecast-accuracy test passes (t = −2.13), the advantage is era-stable (same sign in both halves, significant in the recent half), and tracking error is better, not worse. No kill trigger fires. The claim that advances is exactly and only: implied-vol forecasting improves monthly volatility-targeting of a Bitcoin position relative to trailing realized vol — better forecast, tighter targeting, lower drawdown and cost. No directional claim. The study is packaged and ready for the binding validation pass (Section 14).

11. How to use it — operating rules (entry & exit)

The signal predicts how much Bitcoin will move, not which way. It therefore is not a buy/sell trigger; it is a sizing and risk rule. There are two concrete ways to operate it, both stated in full below.

11.1 Operating rule A — volatility-targeted sizing (the backtested rule)

Forecast. On the 1st of each month, read the Deribit 30-day ATM implied volatility (annualized, e.g. 55 = 55%); call it IV. This is the volatility forecast for the coming month.
Size. Set position size as a fraction of capital:  weight = min( target_vol / IV , 1.5 )  with target_vol = 0.30 (a 30% annualized risk target; cap at 1.5× capital).
Entry. Establish/adjust the position to that weight at the close of the first trading day on or after the 1st.
Exit / rebalance. Hold until the next month-start, then recompute IV and resize. There is no stop and no directional exit — the rule only scales exposure. Lower implied vol → larger position; higher implied vol → smaller position.
Worked example. If month-start IV = 60%, weight = 0.30/0.60 = 0.50 (half capital). If IV = 40%, weight = 0.75. If IV = 20%, weight = min(1.50, 1.5) = 1.50 (capped).

This is exactly the rule tested in Section 9. It is venue-agnostic risk sizing; the monthly hold itself is not intended for an intraday-flat execution account — its real use is Rule B.

11.2 Operating rule B — volatility-regime gate for an executable strategy

Read. Track the implied-vol level as a rolling percentile (e.g. its rank over the trailing 12 months).
Gate / scale. When implied vol is high (e.g. top tercile), reduce the size of, or stand down, an executable intraday strategy; when low, run normal or larger size — because high implied vol forecasts high realized vol (larger swings, wider stops, worse risk-adjusted fills), and low implied vol forecasts calm.
Entry/exit of the host strategy are unchanged — this rule only modulates how much the host strategy risks, using the implied-vol forecast as the risk dial.

Rule B is the deployable use on an intraday-flat execution account: the conditioner sizes/gates a strategy that is executable, rather than holding a monthly position itself.

11.3 Where it adds value

In both modes the result is a conditioner, not a directional strategy — directional use is correctly dead, because the implied-minus-realized gap is a risk premium, not a direction forecast. Its value is in improving other decisions:

  • Regime / volatility-gate input. Feed the implied-vol level (or its rolling percentile) into a volatility-regime gate that decides when and how large an executable strategy should trade, benchmarked against the cheaper trailing-RV gate it outperforms.
  • Position sizing / volatility targeting. Demonstrated directly in Study 2 — a +0.66-IC forward-vol forecast sizes tighter and cheaper than the conventional estimate.
  • Risk overlay. Use the implied-vol level to scale aggregate risk up or down ahead of realized-vol expansions, which it anticipates.
  • Volatility-premium screen. When the implied-minus-realized gap is unusually wide, short-volatility structures are statistically favored — subject to options-venue feasibility, which has not yet been assessed, and noting the premium’s sign for Bitcoin is sensitive to how realized vol is measured (Section 12).

Strategic value. 19V’s genuine edges have repeatedly proven locked to a specific execution venue. A venue-agnostic conditioner that sharpens the sizing and timing of whatever is executable is exactly the kind of reusable component that survives a change of venue — which is why it is worth banking even before the final validation pass.

12. What is interesting / non-obvious

  • The forecast is unusually strong — treated as a flag, not just a win. Comparable equity studies explain 30–50% of volatility variation; +0.66 sits at the top of that range. The decisive guard against an inflated number is the test in Section 8.2 (implied vol subsumes trailing vol), which rules out the “it’s just persistence” explanation. Trailing vol alone already scores +0.50 here, so +0.66 for implied vol is in-family, not anomalous.
  • The directional kill is the textbook outcome, not a disappointment. Decades of variance-risk-premium research show the implied-minus-realized gap is compensation for bearing volatility risk — it predicts volatility, not direction. Finding exactly that in Bitcoin is reassuring corroboration.
  • Bias-correcting the forecast backfired (Section 9.3) — a clean, counter-intuitive lesson: at small samples, “correcting” a known bias overfits and destroys the signal. The raw, untouched forecast is best.
  • Bitcoin’s volatility premium has the opposite sign to equities. Measured naively the premium looks positive (+3.9 vol points), but because realized vol here is computed on weekday futures hours while implied vol references a 24/7 market, the weekend-adjusted premium is roughly −5.6 vol points — Bitcoin implied vol trades cheap relative to true round-the-clock realized vol. The premium’s level is therefore definition-dependent; only the rank relationships were relied upon.
  • History warned us about exactly this setup. An influential 1993 study found stock-index implied vol had near-zero predictive power; a 1998 study showed that was an artifact of overlapping data windows, and that implied vol is in fact an efficient forecast. Our 21-day windows overlap in the same way — which is precisely why the Newey-West correction and the era-stability checks are doing essential work here.

14.1 Known traps to control for

  • Overlapping-observation bias — inflates significance on overlapping forward windows; handled here via Newey-West, worth a non-overlapping cross-check.
  • Errors-in-variables in implied vol (bid-ask noise, stale quotes, interpolation) — biases regression slopes; the thin historical archive amplifies it, and is the likely cause of the bias-correction backfire (Section 9.3).
  • Jump / fat-tail concentration — confirm the forecast is not carried by a handful of extreme months (the scatter in Section 8.1 suggests it is not).
  • Realized-vol measurement choice — sampling frequency and the weekday-vs-24/7 convention materially change the target and drive the premium-level ambiguity (Section 12); document and stress-test.

15. Limitations & validation status

Read before drawing conclusions.
  • Research fidelity, not validated. Both studies are first-pass. The binding out-of-sample / walk-forward / selection-bias validation (Section 14, step 1) has not been run. Treat all numbers as research-grade.
  • Small sample. 60–61 monthly observations. The headline forecast is robust at this n; anything Sharpe-shaped is not, and is reported as illustrative only.
  • Monthly resolution. The archive supports monthly-horizon claims only; nothing intraday or weekly can be concluded until the daily collectors mature.
  • One macro cycle. The data spans roughly one full Bitcoin market cycle; era-stability is established within that window, not beyond it.
  • Reused data window. This Bitcoin sample has been examined multiple times for directional ideas; while the volatility target tested here is a different quantity, further re-use of the same window risks exhausting its statistical independence. Genuinely fresh or rigorously held-out data is preferred for the binding pass.
  • Premium level is definition-dependent. The variance-risk-premium level depends on how realized vol is measured (weekday vs 24/7); only rank relationships were relied upon.

16. Conclusion

What it is. The month-start 30-day at-the-money implied volatility of Deribit Bitcoin options is a strong, reliable forecast of how volatile Bitcoin will be over the following month. It is the one genuinely positive, reproducible result from 19V’s Bitcoin research effort — everything else tested was rejected.

What was proven. Two independent studies. The first established the forecast itself: rank correlation +0.66 with forward realized volatility, significant after the strictest multiple-testing correction, stable across every market era observed, and — the decisive check — it statistically absorbs the conventional backward-looking volatility estimate, proving it carries genuinely forward-looking information rather than just extrapolating recent calm or turbulence. The second study put the forecast to work sizing a position and showed it does the job better than the conventional estimate: more accurate, it tracks a risk target 13–20% more tightly, runs about 35% cheaper, and roughly halves drawdown versus an unsized position. The directional question — can it predict which way Bitcoin moves — was tested and answered no, exactly as theory predicts.

How it helps us. It is a volatility/risk dial, not a buy/sell signal. Use it (Section 11) to size positions and to gate executable strategies: ease off risk when it forecasts high volatility, lean in when it forecasts calm. Because it is a venue-agnostic risk tool rather than a standalone trade, it remains useful even as the strategies it supports change — making it durable, reusable research infrastructure rather than a single perishable edge.

The one caveat. Everything here is research-grade. The result is strong and internally consistent, but the final binding validation — a full out-of-sample and walk-forward pass on fresh data — has not yet been run. That is the immediate next step, after which the conditioner can be wired into live sizing decisions.

Recommendation. Preserve and bank this result; adopt the raw implied-vol forecast (no bias-correction) as a volatility-sizing and regime-gating input; and schedule the binding validation pass before it informs capital at risk.

Bottom line. Deribit month-start 30-day ATM implied volatility is a strong, era-stable, literature-consistent forecast of Bitcoin’s forward realized volatility (IC +0.66) that statistically absorbs the conventional trailing-vol estimate, and it sizes a position measurably better than that estimate (forecast-loss advantage, tighter volatility targeting, roughly halved drawdown). It is a volatility/risk conditioner, not directional alpha — the directional use was tested and correctly rejected. The immediate next step is the binding validation pass; until then every figure here is research-grade. The references in Section 13 marked “verify DOI” are standard, heavily-cited works worth a brief citation check before formal use.
